# Basic words and phrases

## Introduction

Pandunia is a constructed language that is designed to be relatively easy for
everyone. You can learn it fast with this practical course.

English speakers will find it easy to make basic sentences in Pandunia as
the word order is generally the same as in English,
there are no definite or indefinite articles,
and no complicated rules about changing the form of words to express singular and plural or the tense of verbs.

The course consists of short lessons.
Each lesson introduces one new word, which is used in several different phrases in the lesson.
This is to teach you how the word works as part of sentences.
Possibly you will encounter also other new words in the same lesson but don't worry about them!
You don't have to learn all of them at once.
Just memorize the phrases that are useful for you!
Maybe the rest will go to your memory subconciously.

You can study this course together with one or several friends.
Read the phrases together and try to make small conversations.
You can also study alone.
Even then it's useful to read out loud and create conversations.
Repeat the same phrases several times today, tomorrow, the day after tomorrow and so on.
As they say, _repetition is the mother of learning_.

Note! Many lessons include also tips and notes like this.
They are there to clarify grammatical details for those who are interested.
You can skip over them if they are not helpful.
You don't have to know the theory of the language.
You can just speak Pandunia!


## Part 1: Greeting and basics

### sal <small>greet, greeting</small>

<p style="font-size:5em;">🙋‍</p>

*sal!*
– Hello!

*sal suba!*
– Good morning!

*sal dia!*
– Good day!

*sal sham!*
– Good evening!

*sal noce!*
– Good night!

*sal yam!*
– Bon appetit!

*sal vene!*
– Welcome!

*sal cut!*
– Goodbye!

*sal safar!*
– Have a safe journey!

*sal sona!*
– Sleep well!

As you can see from the range of expressions,
*sal*
is a general word for well-wishing.
Use it any time!
You can also say
*halo*
'hello, hi'
or
*salam*
'greetings'
when you greet someone.

<small>Similar words to
*sal*
in other languages:  
French *salut*,
Arabic سَلَام‏ (salām),
Hebrew
שָׁלוֹם‏ (šalom),
Turkish *selam*,
Hindi सलाम (salām),
Swahili *salaam*,
Indonesian *selamat*.</small>


### shukre <small>thanks</small>

<p style="font-size:5em;">🙏</p>

*shukre!*
– Thanks!

*multi shukre!*
– Thanks a lot!

*ya, shukre.*
– Yes, thank you.

*ne, shukre.*
– No, thank you.

*shukre tu.*
– Thank you.

*mi shukre tu.*
– I thank you.

*mi shukre tu helpe mi.*
– I thank you for helping me.

*shukre tu helpe mi.*
– Thanks for helping me.

*ne yau shukre.*
– You're welcome. (Literally: _No need to thank._)


### es <small>to be</small>

The word
*es*
is used for stating something as a fact.

*mi es Tomas.*
– I am Thomas.

*tu es Sara.*
– You are Sarah.

*aple es frute.*
– The apple is a fruit.

<small>Similar words to
*es*
in other languages:  
Spanish *es*,
English *is*,
German *ist*,
French *est*.</small>


### mi <small>I, me</small>

*mi es Sara.*
– I'm Sarah.

*mi es Tomas.*
– I'm Thomas.

*mi sal mama.*
– I greet mother.

*mi sal papa.*
– I greet father.

The word
*sal*
is both a noun and a verb.
The basic word order in Pandunia is subject–verb–object.

*Sara ya sal Tomas.*
– Sarah greets Thomas.

*sal mama!*
– Greetings, mother!

*sal papa!*
– Greetings, father!

<small>Similar words to
*me*
in other languages:  
English *me*,
Spanish *mí*,
Portuguese *me*,
French *me*,
Hindi: मैं (meṇ),
Fulfulde *mi*,
Swahili *mimi*.</small>


### tu <small>you</small>

*tu es Tomas.*
– You are Thomas.

*mi sal tu.*
– I greet you.

*tu sal mi.*
– You greet me.

Pronouns don't ever change their form in Pandunia.
That's why
*mi*
is the same in subject and object positions
while English has two different forms, 'I' and 'me'.

*mi love tu.*
– I love you.

<small>Similar words to
*tu*
in other languages:  
Spanish *tu*,
Italian *tu*,
French *tu*,
Russian ты (ty),
Hindi तू (tū),
Persian تو‏‎ (to),
Tajik ту (tu).</small>


### he <small>he, she or it</small>

<p style="font-size:4em;">👥🗣️👉👤</p>

*he es man.*
– He is a man.

*he es fem.*
– She is a woman.

*he es aple.*
– It is an apple.

*he*
is the general third person pronoun.
It is used for people (irrespective of gender) as well as for things.

*mi sal he.*
– I greet him/her.

<small>Similar words to
*he*
in other languages:  
Hebrew הוּא /hu/,
Arabic هو /howa/,
Turkish *o*,
Fulani *o*,
Yoruba *ó*,
Persian او /u/,
Hindi वह /voh/.
</small>


### sual <small>to ask a question</small>

*sual tu es Tomas?*
– Are you Thomas?

*sual tu es dokter?*
– Are you a doctor?

Tip: Yes/no questions frequently begin with
*sual*.
It is just a regular verb, not a special _question tag_.
In fact, the previous question is simply abbreviated from
*mi sual tu es dokter*
(I ask, you are doctor?) by dropping out the first word.

*sual tu hao?*
– How are you? (Literally: _Are you good?_)  
*mi hao.*
– I'm good.  
*tu ne?*
– And you?  
*mi ne hao.*
– I'm not good.

<small>Etymology. *sual* is from
Arabic سؤال (su'āl),
Hindi सवाल (savāl),
Malay *soal*,
Swahili *swali*.</small>


### ya <small>yes</small>

*sual tu es Tomas?*
– Are you Thomas?

*ya, mi es Tomas.*
– Yes, I am Thomas.

*sual he es dokter?*
– Is he/she a doctor?

*ya, he es dokter.*
– Yes, he is a doctor.

<small>Similar words to
*ya*
in other languages:  
German *ja*,
English *yeah*,
Malay *ya*,
Korean 예 (ya).</small>


### ne <small>no, not</small>

*mi ne es Sara.*
– I'm not Sarah.

*mi ne es dokter.*
– I'm not a doctor.

*sual tu hao?*
– Are you well?  
*ya. mi hao.*
– Yes, I'm well.

*sual tu es hao?*
– Are you well?  
*ne, mi ne es hao.*
– No, I'm not well.

You can use
*ne*
to deny anything.
It is placed before the word that is denied.

*he ne sal mi.*
– He/she doesn't greet me.

<small>Similar words to
*ne*
in other languages:  
Spanish *no*,
English *no*,
French *non*,
Persian نه⁩ (na),
Bengali না (na).</small>


### Plural personal pronouns

| Singular    | Plural       |
|:------------|:-------------|
| *mi*      | *wi*    |
| I, me       | we           |
| *tu*      | *yu*    |
| you         | you all      |
| *he*      | *de*    |
| he or she   | they         |


*wi sal yu.*
– We greet you all.

*yu sal de.*
– You greet them.

*de sal wi.*
– They greet us.

*wi es fem.*
– We are women.

*yu es man.*
– You are men.

*de es aple.*
– They are apples.


### wa person? <small>who?</small>

*wa person?*
– Who? ~ Which person?

*tu es wa person?*
– Who are you?  
*mi es Tomas.*
– I'm Thomas.

*he es wa person?*
– Who is he/she?  
*he es Sara.*
– She is Sarah.

*yu es wa person?*
– Who are you people?

*de es wa person?*
– Who are they?


### wa ting? <small>what?</small>

*wa ting?*
– What?

*he es wa ting?*
– What is that?

*he es buk.*
– That is a book.

<small>Similar words to
*wa*
in other languages:  
English *what*,
German *was*,
Dutch *wat*,
Swedish *vad*.
</small>


### ye <small>this</small>

*ye es wa ting?*
– What's this?

*ye es aple.*
– This is an apple.

*he es rubi aple.*
– It is a red apple.

*de sta wa plas?*
– Where are they?

*de sta ye.*
– They are here.


### vo <small>that</small>

*vo es wa ting?*
– What is that?

*vo es aple.*
– That is an apple.

*da aple es rubi.*
– The apple is red.

*da es rubi.*
– It is red.

*de sta wa plas?*
– Where are they?

*de sta da plas.*
– They are there.


### oun <small>own, 's</small>

*ye es wa ting?*
– What's this?

*he es mi telefon.*
– It's my telephone.

*ye es wa oun?*
– Whose is this?

*he es mi oun.*
– It's mine.

Note: The possessive particle
*oun*
is joined to the owner. So
*mi oun*
means 'my',
*tu oun*
means 'your' and so on.

*he es wa person?*
– Who's he/she?

*he es mi oun doste.*
– He/she is my friend.

*mi es Sara oun doste.*
– I am Sarah's friend.

It's also possible to end a sentence with
*oun*.

*mau es mi oun.*
The cat is mine.  
*mone es wi oun.*
The money is our.  
*doste es Sara oun.*
The friend is Sara's.

<small>
Similar words to
*oun*
in other languages:
English *oun*.</small>


### nim <small>name</small>

*tu nim es wa?*
– What's your name?

*mi nim es Tomas.*
– My name is Thomas.

*he nim es wa?*
– What is his/her name?

*he nim es Sara.*
– Her name is Sarah.

<small>Similar words to
*nim*
in other languages:
Hindi नाम  (nām),
Farsi نام‏‎ (nām),
Thai นาม (naam),
Malay *nama*,

### have <small>have</small>

*mi have un hao haus.*
– I have a good house.

*he ne have mone.*
– He doesn't have money.

*mi voli have un nova telefon.*
– I want to have a new telephone.

*sual tu have pikin?*
– Do you have children?  
*mi have du pikin.*
– I have two children.

*sual have dokter?*
– Is there a doctor?  
*ya. he es dokter.*
– Yes. He or she is a doctor.

*sual have vin?*
– Do you have wine?  
*ya. baki e rubi.*
– Yes, white and red.


### nos <small>know</small>

*mi nos he.*
– I know him/her/it.

*sual tu nos vo jen?*
– Do you know that person?

*mi ne nos.*
– I don't know.

*sual yu nos alale?*
– Do you know each other?

*wi nos alale zi long.*
– We know each other for a long time.


### helpe <small>help</small>

*mi yau helpe.*
– I need help.

*sual tu pote helpe mi?*
– Can you help me?

*sual mi pote helpe tu?*
– Can I help you?

*mi voli helpe tu.*
– I want to help you.


## Part 2: Eating

### yam <small>consume, eat, drink</small>

*sual tu voli yam som made?*
– Would you like to eat something?

*he yam un aple.*
– He/she eats an apple.

*de yam aple.*
– They eat apples.

Note: Unlike English, Pandunia doesn't have separate singular and plural forms.
Therefore a word like
*aple*
can refer to one or more apples.

*sual tu yam kafe?*
– Do you drink coffee?

*ya. mi yam kafe.*
– Yes, I drink coffee.

Tip: Meaning of
*yam*
covers both eating and drinking.
It can feel odd at first but soon you will see that it is quite handy!
Usually the object of the verb tells is it about eating, drinking or both.

*mi yam kafe e ban.*
– I'm having coffee and bread.


### voli <small>want</small>

*sual tu voli yam?*
– Would you like to eat?

*tu voli yam wa ting?*
– What would you like to eat?

Tip: While English puts the "what" at the beginning of a question, in Pandunia
the word order is not affected by the
*wa ting*.

*mi voli yam kafe.*
– I want to drink coffee.

*sual tu voli yam ca?*
– Would you like to drink tea?  
*ne. mi ne voli ca. mi voli kafe.*
– No, I don't want tea. I want coffee.

*tu voli wa aple?*
– Which apple do you want?  
*he rubi.*
– This red one.


### ples <small>ask, beg; please</small>

*mi ples tu yam ca.*
– I beg you to drink tea.

*mi ples tu vene haus.*
– I beg you to come home.

*mi ples tu helpe mi.*
– I beg you to help me.

Tip: To make direct requests, drop all the pronouns.

*ples yam ca.*
– Please, have some tea!

*ples yam kafe.*
– Please, have some coffee!

*ples vene haus.*
– Please, come home!

*ples helpe mi.*
– Please, help me.


### shal <small>let's, shall</small>

*wi shal yam!*
– Let's eat!

*yu shal yam!*
– You all shall eat!

*tu shal yam!*
– You shall eat!

*yam!*
– Eat!

*wi shal go yam!*
– Let's go eat!

*wi shal go haus.*
– Let's go home.


### yau <small>need</small>

*mi yau helpe.*
– I need help.

*mi yau yam.*
– I'm hungry.

*sual tu yau yam?*
– Are you hungry?

*sual tu yau sui?*
– Are you thirsty?


## Part 3. Communication

### perdon <small>sorry, pardon</small>

*perdon! mi ne aha.*
– Sorry, I don't understand.

*perdon! ye es wa ting?*
– Excuse me, what's this?

*perdon. tu nim es wa?*
– Excuse me, what's your name?


### aha <small>understand</small>

*sual tu aha mi?*
– Do you understand me?

*mi aha.*
– I understand.

*perdon. mi ne aha tu.*
– Sorry. I don't understand you.

*mi ne bas aha tu.*
– I didn't quite understand you.

*mi aha no made.*
– I don't understand at all.


### pote <small>can</small>

*mi pote cofe kar.*
– I can drive a car.

*he ne pote cofe kar.*
– He/she doesn't know how to drive a car.

*sual tu pote shuli he?*
– Do you know how to fix it?

*sual tu pote pandunia?*
– Can you speak Pandunia?

*mi pote pandunia.*
– I can speak Pandunia.

*mi pote kam pandunia.*
– I can speak a little Pandunia.

*mi ne pote angle.*
– I can't speak English.

*perdon. mi ne pote tu basa.*
– Sorry, I can't speak your language.


### log <small>to say, speak, talk</small>

*tu log wa ting?*
– What did you say?

*mi log do tu.*
– I talk to you.

*wi log do alale.*
– We talk to each other.

*bi loge*
**cat**
*na wa mode na pandunia?*
– How do you say "cat" in Pandunia?

**cat**
*es wa na pandunia?*
– What is "cat" in Pandunia?

*mau loge "miau".*
– Cat says "meow".


### audi <small>to listen, hear</small>

*audi mi!*
– Listen to me!

*mi ne pote audi tu.*
– I can't hear you.

*ples log mor bala.*
– Please speak louder.

*mi audi muzike.*
– I listen to music.

*tu audi wa tip da muzike?*
– What kind of music do you listen to?


### vize <small>to see</small>

*suka vize tu.*
– Pleased to see you!

*do rivize!*
– See you again!

*vize tu a posden!*
– See you tomorrow!

*mi vize he a preden.*
– I saw him/her yesterday.


### sema <small>to mean</small>

*ye lexe ya sema wa ting?*
– What does this word mean?

*"mau" ya sema wa ting?*
– What does "mau" mean?

*he sema un tipe zi hevan.*
– It means a kind of animal.

*mi ne aha he sema wa ting.*
– I don't understand what it means.


### kitab <small>write</small>

*ples kitab tu adres.*
– Please, write your address.

*ples kitab he a ye.*
– Please, write it here!


### basa <small>speak a language, communicate</small>

*sual yu basa pandunia.*
– Do you speak Pandunia?

*wi basa pandunia.*
– We speak Pandunia.

*sual tu pote basa angle?*
– Can you speak English?

*franse, espanya, portugal, rus*
– French, Spanish, Portuguese, Russian

*putong han, nipon, malayu*
– Chinese, Japanese, Malay

*arabe, turke, parse, urdu, hindi*
– Arabic, Turkish, Farsi, Urdu, Hindi

*suahili, hausa, yoruba, amara*
– Swahili, Hausa, Yoruba, Amharic


## Part 4. Going around

### go <small>to go</small>

*go do vo!*
– Go there!

*tu go wa plas?*
– Where are you going?

*mi go haus.*
– I'm going home.

*mi mus go wa plas?*
– Where should I go?

*mi mus go do wa person?*
– To whom should I go?

*wi shal go!*
– Let's go!

*wi shal go a fut*
– Let's go by foot!


### vene <small>to come</small>

*vene ye!*
– Come here!

*ples vene!*
– Please come!

*tu vene zi wa plas?*
– Where do you come from?

*mi vene zi Dubai.*
– I come from Dubai.

*mi vene haus a pos den.*
– I will come home tomorrow.


### safar <small>to travel</small>

*sual tu safar a tren?*
– Do you travel by train?

*wi safar zi London do Paris.*
– We travel from London to Paris.

*safar es muce long.*
– The voyage is very long.


### a <small>in, on, at</small>

*hotel a wa plas?*
– Where is the hotel?

*hotel a vo dao.*
– The hotel is on that road.

*tu a wa plas?*
– Where are you?

*mi a haus.*
– I'm at home.

*he a wa plas?*
– Where is he/she?

*he sid a kamar.*
– He/she sits in the room.

Tip! You can use
*a*
as a preposition or alone as the verb.

*mi verke a...*
– I work at ...


### loju <small>to live, reside</small>

*tu loju in wa plas?*
– Where do you live in?

*mi loju Singapur.*
– I live in Singapore.

*sual tu loju ye hotel?*
– Do you live in this hotel?


### dele <small>to wait</small>

*ples dele!*
– Please wait!

*dele mi!*
– Wait for me!

*de dele wi.*
– They wait for us.

*mi dele tu a hotel.*
– I wait for you in the hotel.


## Part 5. Time expressions

### bi <small>currently</small>

*mi bi sal tu doste.*
– I am greeting your friend.

*he man bi vize un filme.*
– The man is watching a film.

*he bi es cef.*
– He or she is being the chief.

*he bi es a haus.*
– He or she is currently at home.


### hav <small>already, completed</small>

*mi hav sal tu doste.*
– I have greeted your friend.

*he man hav vize he filme.*
– The man has watched the film.

*he hav es cef.*
– He has been the chief.

*he hav es a haus.*
– He has been at home.


### le <small>in the past</small>

*mi le sal tu doste.*
– I greeted your friend.

*man le vize un filme.*
– The man watched a film.

*he le es cef.*
– He/she was the chief.

*he le es a haus.*
– He/she was at home.


### fu <small>in the future</small>

*mi fu sal tu doste.*
– I will greet your friend.

*he man fu vize he filme.*
– The man will watch the film.

*he fu es cef.*
– He/she will be the chief.

*he fu es a haus.*
– He/she will be at home.



## Part 6. Doing business

### don <small>give</small>

*ples don mone.*
– Please give some money.

*ples don he do mi.*
– Please give it to me.

*mi don ye do tu.*
– I give this to you.

*mi don buk do de.*
– I give a book to them.

*he ne voli don he do mi.*
– He/she doesn't want to give it to me.


### cepe <small>take, get</small>

*mi cepe un kafe.*
– I will take a coffee.

*ples cepe un kafe do mi.*
– Please take one coffee for me.

*tu le cepe mone zi mi.*
– You already got money from me before.

*mi cepe un bir.*
– I will take a beer.


### kira <small>rent, lease, hire</small>

*kira es 500 dolar a mes.*
– The rent is 500 dollars in a month.

*he ne pote pei he kira.*
– He/she can't pay the rent.

*mi voli cepe un kar a kira.*
– I want to take a car for rent.

*mi voli cepe kira un kar.*
– I want to rent a car.

*he don kira kamar do safarer.*
– He/she rents rooms to travelers.
